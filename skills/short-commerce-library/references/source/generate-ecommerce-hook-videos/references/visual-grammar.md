## Selection Rule

Choose visual grammar only after deciding why the viewer stops. Use the lowest-complexity method that preserves the intended impact:

```text
product-native action → camera/performance → editing/optics/light → practical effect → VFX/CG
```

Raise complexity when the product has invisible mechanisms, strong digital semantics, a supplied physical metaphor, or an explicit spectacle request. Use one dominant camera movement and one visual anomaly in five seconds.

## Product-Native Visual Grammar

### Decisive Hand Action

Use grab, tear, pull, twist, snap, throw, catch, cover, point, press, unfold, pour or hand-over. Specify direction, speed, grip and impact; begin on the action rather than preparation.

### Macro And Focus

Turn fibers, food texture, metal joints, switches, droplets, paper embossing, woven loops or coatings into a landscape. Use one decisive focus pull or scale match to reveal the whole product.

### Occlusion And Peak Reveal

Hide information behind a hand, package, cloth, foreground object, doorway, shadow or the product itself. Reveal on the strongest movement, impact, spoken stress, switch, catch or camera turn. Preserve gesture, direction, shape, light, sound or sentence meaning across the reveal.

### Push, Pull, Pan And Camera Transport

- Push in to turn one feature into the event.
- Pull out to reveal quantity, location or scale.
- Use one crash zoom for urgency or realization.
- Use a whip pan for speed or a hidden cut.
- Let a hand, product opening, package, cloth, light or moving object carry the camera into the next segment.

### Optics And Perspective

- Forced perspective: make a small product become architecture, let a hand catch a distant object, or align an indoor product with an outdoor form.
- Reflection/refraction: use mirror, metal, glass, water, lens or glossy packaging as a second image plane.
- Wide-angle proximity: make product or hand invade the viewer's space.
- Dolly zoom: use only for one realization or spatial anomaly.

### Time And Editing

- Slow only the pivotal tear, snap, splash, crumb, whip, unfold or magnetic closure.
- Use one speed ramp to accent contact or reveal.
- Freeze an imminent event while one element continues; treat this as the sole anomaly.
- Reverse one familiar action when the reversal stays immediately readable.
- Use jump cuts for quantity or rhythm, smash cuts for contrast, and match cuts for one continuous shape/action/sound.
- Hide transitions inside a hand, cloth, package, body turn, whip, light fill, shadow or splash.

### Practical Physics And Tabletop

Use precise placement, repeatable camera, real shadows and synchronized sound for drops, magnets, hinges, buttons, latches, chain reactions, package openings, arrays, liquid, ice, bubbles, crumbs, fabric and paper.

Keep cause and effect credible unless one brief anomaly is the Hook. Avoid prolonged levitation, deformation or impossible hand-object contact.

### Composition, Light And Performance

- Use quantity, symmetry, controlled disorder, color blocking or one object breaking a pattern.
- Use light switch, sweep, shadow-before-object, projection, silhouette or lens fill.
- Use POV and direct gaze to hand, fit, throw, cover or move the product toward the viewer.
- Use result-first when the finished room, outfit, projection, light or atmosphere is already the spectacle.

## Universal Hook Chassis

A chassis is reusable attention choreography, not a finished product idea. Rebuild its peak for the current product.

### Extreme Entrance

Start already descending, rising, launching, dropping, swinging, being pulled or blown into frame. Preserve the motion vector through the product reveal.

### Environment Takeover And Static Anchor

Keep one product or product-shaped anchor stable while the surrounding room, street, table, paper, fabric, light or particles reorganize around it. Let the takeover originate from a product action or property.

### Camera Or Viewer Impact

Let an object, hand, wave, cloth, package, light or shadow strike, cover, pull or pass through the lens. Reveal the product as the impact source or object received by the viewer.

### Scale Inversion

Move from tiny to architectural, one to many, product to environment, or foreground illusion to full-scale reveal. Preserve one recognizable product state at the peak.

### Aperture Or Open-Close Trigger

Use a lid, package, door, iris, earcup, zipper, cloth fold, lens or product opening as the switch that changes the whole frame.

### Reverse Familiar Ritual

Make one known action run backward or resolve unexpectedly: fragments reassemble, liquid returns, a falling object rises, an expected object is replaced, or a routine begins from its result.

### Format Hijack

Begin as a generic tutorial, interview, video call, commentary, fictional bulletin, airport-style announcement or surveillance view, then let the product break the format. Do not imitate real platforms, outlets, institutions or authorities.

### Strong Motion Vector

Use one unmistakable direction—vertical plunge, lateral sweep, forward collision, spiral pull, expanding ring or rapid accumulation—and keep sound, camera and reveal aligned to it.

### Chain Reaction

Let one product-relevant trigger cause no more than two or three clearly ordered consequences. The last consequence is the product peak, not a second spectacle.

### Fourth-Wall Interaction

Let a person or object tap, cover, grab, move, look through or hand something toward the lens. Avoid fake buttons or deceptive interface elements.

## Product-Specific Peak Binding

Allow the chassis to remain reusable. Reject the execution when the product is only a disconnected Packshot.

At the peak, make the product at least one of:

- **stimulus**: its click, tear, switch, impact or light causes the event
- **action object**: it is caught, opened, worn, pulled, passed, assembled or struck
- **anomaly cause**: its shape, name, material or mechanism explains the abnormality
- **answer**: it completes the image, sentence, reaction, format or mystery
- **result anchor**: the environment change points back to it as the visible cause

Test the binding by swapping in an unrelated product. The chassis may still work, but the peak choreography, motion, sound or semantic answer must need rewriting. If nothing needs rewriting, strengthen the binding.

## VFX Patterns

Use one family:

- invisible mechanism visualization: sound, signal, data, energy, magnetism, light
- component explode and reassembly
- scale inversion or giant product
- spatial portal or camera travel
- controlled particles or material morph
- one environment response to one product action
- FOOH or frame-breaking spectacle
- tracked graphic or mixed-media layer

For consumer electronics, prefer:

```text
real operation → invisible mechanism becomes visible → environment responds once
```

Keep VFX expressive, not evidentiary. Do not visualize unsupported waterproofing, durability, charging speed, battery life, latency, health or comparative performance.

## Anti-Showcase Blacklist

Reject or rewrite these before scoring unless they occur only as a brief post-peak hold:

- slow push toward a static product
- product floating in soft light with no rupture
- sequential display of package angles or colorways
- ordinary unboxing with a slow opening
- gentle particles, glow or orbiting lines used as the only event
- generic lifestyle establishing footage followed by a Packshot
- several expensive effects with no single attention vector
- product visible from frame one but no later peak exceeds it

Heavy Hook comes from a clear delta—speed, scale, direction, sound, meaning, state or viewpoint—not from the number of effects.

## Seedance Feasibility

Prefer one product, one dominant hand/person, one camera move, one material behavior, one anomaly and no more than three meaningful states.

Use carefully: dense stop-motion, exact match cuts, many identical branded packages, complex component views, mirrors, multiple hands, multiple locations and readable generated text.

Simplify when the plan changes packaging facts, hides the product for the entire peak, requires missing assets, combines unrelated climaxes or makes the peak a fast blur without a stable recognizable product frame.
