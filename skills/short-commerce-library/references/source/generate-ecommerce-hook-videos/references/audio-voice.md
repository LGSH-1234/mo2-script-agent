## Sound As Attention Attack

Use sound or speech as a Hook only when the audible event itself can stop attention or sharpen the visual strike. Do not append generic audio after the visual idea is finished.

Distinguish:

- **non-verbal sound** supplies attack, texture, rhythm, space and contrast
- **language** supplies meaning, conflict and information gap

When wording carries the Hook, also read [Semantic Hooks](semantic-hooks.md).

Replace `撕开包装，搭配有质感的 ASMR` with an executable event: begin on a forceful two-hand plastic tear; use a close-miked, immediate, dry, high-frequency `刺啦` with no reverb tail; land the package on the table with one dense `啪` at the reveal.

## Sound-Event Specification

For every important sound, define only the useful subset:

- **source**: plastic, paper, fabric, metal, button, magnet, liquid, food, glass, environment, voice
- **action**: tear, snap, click, impact, rub, twist, pour, crack, slide, lock, stop
- **attack**: immediate, sharp, soft, rising, interrupted
- **tone**: high, low, dry, crisp, dense, hollow, metallic, granular
- **distance**: close-miked, arm's length, room, distant
- **rhythm**: single, repeated, accelerating, count-synced, broken once
- **tail**: no reverb, short decay, resonant ring, low-frequency aftershock, sudden silence
- **sync**: exact visible action or spoken stress where it lands

Specify enough to prevent an ordinary, slow or mismatched result; do not fill every property mechanically.

## Useful Acoustic Arcs

### Transient Impact

Use tear, snap, click, magnetic lock, cap pop, break, slap, drop, latch, plug or switch. Start on the attack, not the preparation.

### Continuous Texture

Use paper pull, fabric rub, squeeze, liquid flow, powder, bubbles, scrape, roll or fizz. Give the texture a decisive start and stop; avoid slow, formless handling.

### Rhythmic Escalation

Repeat one credible sound faster, closer or heavier. Break the rhythm once at product reveal.

### Silence Contrast

Create one credible noisy state, then cut sharply to silence or one intimate product sound. Use silence as punctuation, not empty duration.

### Spatial Movement

Move sound far-to-near, left-to-right, around the viewer or through a product opening only when the visible motion supports it.

### Sound-Image Mismatch

Give a small product one surprising but coherent acoustic scale, or bridge one sound across matched images. Avoid arbitrary comedy sounds that weaken product quality.

## Voice Performance

Give voice one function:

- count and escalate
- interrupt or command
- question or contradict
- create a secret with one pause
- establish a challenge without proving it
- react with a specific realization
- literalize a grounded phrase
- name the product at the visual peak

Specify speaker only when relevant, then define delivery, pace, stressed word, distance and relationship to camera.

Useful performance modes include rapid count, dry disbelief, sudden shout, clipped command, controlled whisper, playful chant and calm interruption. Prefer short words, repetition, contrast and a final accented beat. Avoid adjective-heavy description and imitation of a real person's voice, catchphrase, accent or signature performance.

## Synchronization And Mix

Build one hierarchy:

1. primary sound event or voice
2. one supporting material sound or ambience
3. optional music pulse only when it does not mask the Hook

Make the strongest transient, silence cut or verbal stress coincide with the visual peak. If sound is primary, remove full speech or keep one cue. If language is primary, use only one supporting material sound instead of a competing ASMR sequence.

For count rhythm, synchronize each word with a visible appearance or impact; increase speed or weight; insert a half-beat break; land the final line and product reveal together.

## Failure Patterns

Reject or rewrite:

- slow package opening when the idea needs a sharp tear
- “有 ASMR 感” without a credible source, attack, texture or rhythm
- generic music used as the main stopping device
- voice that merely narrates visible product handling
- voice, music, ASMR, ambience and VFX all competing at full level
- a transient that lands before or after the visible contact
- a flat read with no pace, stress, distance or performance direction
- long subtitles or dialogue that consume the five seconds

Spoken dialogue may make a video model generate unwanted subtitles. When exact subtitle absence matters, prefer post-produced voice or a non-verbal sound-led Hook. Treat exact promotional language under [Safety Boundaries](safety-boundaries.md).
