
# Marketing Proof Playbook

Use this guide when researching a direction, designing StoryMaster, making a Direction concrete, deriving VideoSpec materials, or writing the complete script. Apply only the relevant category examples. They suggest ways to express accepted facts, not new product claims, mandatory shots, or a fixed story structure.

## Design One Readable Proof

Start with the buyer's actual concern, not a list of attractive product features. Sketch:

`Concern -> supported claim -> initial state -> product interaction -> observable result -> practical meaning for the buyer`

For each demonstration, identify what must be visible, the material evidence available, the viewing scale, and enough time to understand the change. A package close-up identifies the product; it does not by itself demonstrate performance. Spoken explanation may interpret a visible result but cannot manufacture one.

Example: for a bag with supported capacity information, show the relevant items beside the bag, their placement, the supported closure state, and retrieval of a needed item. This answers a packing concern more concretely than "large capacity." Do not invent dimensions, stretch the bag, hide extra items through a cut, or turn a generated scene into a claim of real testing.

For comparisons, keep relevant conditions comparable: camera angle, lighting, dose, surface, distance, elapsed time, and subject. Do not use different conditions, filters, or editing to fabricate improvement. Generated illustrations are not empirical evidence, customer testimony, measurements, or proof of an unverified outcome.

Translate qualitative value into behavior where the facts support it:

- Convenience: fewer demonstrated steps, easier retrieval, or a completed task.
- Comfort: natural walking, sitting, or handling in the intended context; not an invented medical result.
- Style: a readable silhouette, proportions, outfit relationship, and suitable occasion.
- Ease of use: clear input, operation, feedback, and finished output.
- Sensory appeal: relevant texture, motion, or sound, without fabricated ingredients or health claims.

If the proposed proof needs an unknown view, state, action, or efficacy claim, identify the missing basis. Use an already supported illustration only if it preserves accepted intent; otherwise follow the existing upstream revision/material choice. Never quietly remove a promised demonstration or generate missing facts to make it work.

## Category Examples

Choose the closest applicable category; these are examples, not a checklist to fill or a reason to create extra assets.

| Category | Buyer concern and useful demonstration | Common failure |
|---|---|---|
| Beauty and personal care | Relevant amount, texture, application, and a supported finish under consistent lighting; explain why the depicted user and use context are appropriate. | A flawless face substituted for evidence; invented instant or long-term effects; changing light between comparisons. |
| Clothing, footwear, bags, accessories | Full silhouette and proportion, standing/walking/turning, relevant fabric movement, on-body scale, or a supported loading/retrieval sequence. | Only flat lays; shoes never shown in motion; a bag with no scale; identity or garment shape drifting between views. |
| Food and drink | Supported serving/preparation state, portion, texture, steam or condensation when appropriate, characteristic sound, then a restrained reaction. | Adjectives and exaggerated delight before sensory evidence; invented contents; impossible pouring or preparation. |
| Home, cleaning, organization | A readable initial problem, correct product interaction, supported result, and the consequence for the person's task. | The problem disappears before the product acts; an unreadably small result; unsafe use or an unsupported guarantee. |
| Electronics and appliances | Actual input, setup/operation, feedback, and output; show what the completed task changes for the user. | A beauty shot replacing operation; invented interface, specifications, or one-click results; output appearing without a cause. |
| Other physical products | Identify the buying concern, one supported physical interaction or attribute, appropriate scale, and a useful completed state. | Generic luxury language, arbitrary creator casting, or effects unrelated to the product. |

Select only demonstrations supported by the exact SKU and sources. A category example never authorizes an unseen internal structure, a new character, a risky action, or a new paid generation call.

## Information And Action Budgets

The full product fact set is not the script's information budget. Choose a primary buying reason and the support the duration can make understandable. Price, usage limits, qualifications, and CTA also take comprehension time even when they are not separate selling points.

Action density is not selling-point density. One proof can remain engaging through changes in scale, contact point, and before/during/after state; each cut need not introduce another claim. Do not automatically append washing, storage, or a static packshot when it contributes nothing to this direction.

Keep the hook's promise connected to the demonstrated result. In a story, the product interaction should cause evidence that changes a character's decision or outcome. In UGC, the creator performs or witnesses the demonstration rather than supplying unsupported praise. In a brand-led piece, an established material, gesture, or sound can carry the final memory without forcing an unrelated hard-sell ending.

For a batch, distribute distinct buying concerns and proof approaches across Directions instead of cramming all of them into every video. Preserve the shared StoryMaster and confirmed exploration boundaries. If user-locked content does not fit, offer a longer complete treatment or a shorter revised treatment for their choice; do not silently cut it, accelerate speech, shorten the finished-video target, or trigger runtime splitting for creative convenience.
