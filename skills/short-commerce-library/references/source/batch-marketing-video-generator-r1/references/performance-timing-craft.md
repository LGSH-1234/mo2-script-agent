
# Performance And Timing Craft

Use this guide when designing the shared story's timing, authoring complete scripts, or translating confirmed content into self-contained part inputs. These are creative judgment aids, not numerical acceptance thresholds. At lowering time, preserve the accepted story, dialogue, and scope.

## Derive Performance From Events

Write the stimulus and what the character wants to do about it, then make that intent visible through gaze, posture, breath, hand movement, action speed, and vocal delivery. Do not prescribe a stock surprise-smile-product-hold sequence or the same emotional arc for every Direction.

Example: instead of "she looks delighted with the organized bag," describe her searching for a needed item, noticing its accessible pocket, retrieving it without disturbing the other contents, and relaxing as she can leave on time. Use this only when the accepted story and product facts support that sequence.

In dialogue, let a line respond to, question, reveal, or change the preceding situation. Give the other person time to perceive the evidence and react. Do not replace confirmed dialogue with narration or add a minimum number of lines merely to make the scene feel dramatic.

## Turn Style Into Executable Choices

Translate the accepted style into camera distance and motion, performance intensity, framing, material/light behavior, editing rhythm, and sound priorities. Put a shot-specific technique in the shot where it serves a purpose, not in a detached list of cinematic terminology.

A handheld everyday treatment and a restrained brand treatment can express the same supported action differently. Neither may change the SKU, factual result, or locked words. Use macro detail, reflections, rim light, or material foley when it reveals something meaningful rather than as automatic decoration.

When an accepted concept uses spectacle or VFX, establish a credible baseline, identify the changed rule, and describe trigger, transition, and visible consequence. Preserve relevant light, shadow, occlusion, scale, and material response. The spectacle should affect the subject or task, not merely put an unchanged product pose in a grand background. Do not imply an unsupported product function.

## Keep Space And States Understandable

Establish the initial arrangement of important people, products, and props. Explain meaningful changes in position, orientation, possession, product state, and sound through actions. After a hard cut to another space, re-establish the relationships needed to understand the next beat.

For an event-dense shot or phase, prioritize a readable main state change; avoid assigning several complex actions to an implausibly short interval. Allocate time for contact, operation, result recognition, and reaction where they matter. Timecodes express intent, not a guarantee of frame-exact generated editing.

Keep continuous actions continuous unless a confirmed cut deliberately changes the view or time. A return to an earlier scene is acceptable when it has a clear narrative purpose; no fixed scene-topology ban applies.

Inside a lowered part, make local action and state transitions self-contained. Preserve the existing part boundaries, local timecodes, required anchors, and handoffs under the lowering contracts. This guidance is not a reason to change the split, add a shot, or reopen creative choices without the normal revision path.

## Budget Speech Separately From Picture Duration

First decide where speech contributes information and where viewers need to watch, hear an operation, or perceive a reaction. Then estimate whether the actual lines fit their assigned intervals in the confirmed content language and intended delivery.

- Spoken coverage and speaking rate are different: intermittent concise speech is not one uninterrupted reading.
- Reserve time for turn-taking, emphasis, breaths, reaction, and necessary sound cues. Allow overlap with action when it remains understandable.
- Check each dense line or exchange, not just the full-script average. Use language-appropriate words/syllables/characters and a realistic pace as an estimate, not a universal conversion or proof of the generated duration.
- Spoken-led, demonstration-led, story-led, and music-led pieces need different balances. Do not impose a fixed percentage or require speech in a deliberately wordless treatment.
- Let visible actions carry what they can. Use speech for useful interpretation, otherwise invisible context, and an applicable accepted CTA, rather than narrating every gesture twice.

If new agent-authored copy is crowded, remove redundant information while retaining the accepted purpose. If user-locked copy or confirmed meaning does not fit, seek a duration/content choice through the existing revision path; do not silently trim, race through it, change the video target, or add calls. Never synthesize actual video dialogue merely to test its duration: this workflow's audio generation remains neutral timbre anchoring only.
