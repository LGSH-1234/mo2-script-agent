
> **Node knowledge:** This reference defines speaking identities, timbre samples, duration, and voice binding in video payloads. PM determines when each voice requirement is ready.

# Voice Anchor Gates

Owns speaking-role consultation, timbre samples and their duration checks, acceptance, speech instructions, voice reuse, and the per-call reference-audio duration budget. PM’s focus package routes these actions here; this reference does not select graph order.

### Speaking-Identity Timbre Anchor Contract

Read the current video's `video_blueprint/<project>/<video>.market_target` before proposing a voice: its content language, audience, market and platform guide the role's delivery within accepted voice instructions. Never use communication language or a sibling video's language as the default. An existing voice can be reused only when its accepted identity, authorization and delivery scope fit this video; a timbre sample alone does not prove a new language or accent. Resolve materially missing target choices through their upstream owner before producing a dependent sample.

First distinguish selection authority from concrete media. A READY audio node declared as voice_sample may close through `delegate-audio` only after explicit user delegation; no sample or subjective audition is required at that point. Auto can remain a textual voice direction in direct execution. In runtime lowering, every used voice needs a concrete asset even if it appears in only one part. PM declares that requirement; follow `runtime-anchor-contract.md` to materialize it once, preserving the original auto fact.

The consultation and listening-acceptance steps below apply when the user retains review of the choice. Runtime `source_auto` / `user_delegated` skips those subjective steps, but retains all objective checks and applicable spending authorization; report through `provide-runtime-anchor`. Runtime `user_review` retains presentation and acceptance. Never call an autonomously chosen sample user-approved. Requirements below to attach every speaking role's anchor apply to concrete anchors; the direct auto exception does not apply to any lowered part.

Treat every recurring speaking identity as a separate voice role: narrator/voiceover, an on-screen character speaking dialogue, a character's inner or direct monologue, and an off-screen character voice all qualify. Describe desired speaking identities while authoring the blueprint; declaration and sample fulfillment happen afterward. Blueprint drafting never waits for voice evidence. One video may contain several speaking identities, and one identity may recur across several videos or independently generated parts. Do not collapse distinct speaking identities into one narrator.

When fulfilling a concrete voice_sample requirement, create and confirm exactly one reusable timbre anchor per distinct speaking identity used by the selected videos, even when that role has only one line or appears in only one call. Distinct characters use distinct anchors by default; reuse one anchor for several roles only when the user explicitly wants the same performer/voice. Do not create one anchor per video when the same identity continues across the batch. A visible character's image anchor and voice anchor are separate requirements: the image fixes appearance, while the audio fixes how that character sounds. An off-screen narrator needs no image anchor, and a silent visible character needs no voice anchor.

Reuse an already accepted neutral user-provided voice anchor only when the user explicitly authorizes it for the mapped speaking role. For a voice_sample requirement, use sandbox_generate_audio solely for neutral TTS timbre anchoring. Separately declared narration/music/ambience requirements have their own semantic purpose and are not subject to this timbre-only content rule. If a required speaking role's timbre is not already explicit, first load `dynamic-questionnaire-guide.md`, then use `dynamic_questionnaire` to confirm concise natural-language `VoiceDesc` values. Before launching the form, visibly present every role and its complete recommended `VoiceDesc`; keep age/gender impression, warmth, energy, texture, accent, **role- and campaign-relevant baseline tone, emotional intensity, and speaking rhythm** in user-facing language. The form may group several roles in one interaction, but it must name each role and provide a closed accept-or-adjust decision without a generic text question. If adjustment is selected, collect role-specific corrections in a focused required follow-up. Per-line emphasis and emotional changes still belong in the actual script.

Do not claim that recommendations follow when the user-visible content omits them. A generic question such as `Do you approve?`, an unlabeled text field, a submit event, or `I have confirmed the above information` does not establish any role's voice direction unless the concrete role-to-`VoiceDesc` mapping was actually shown and accepted. Close a provided audio requirement only with accepted audio paths after that confirmation. Keep the voice-direction gate open and do not call `sandbox_generate_audio` until every required role has accepted consultation evidence.

Generate the anchor under `assets/anchors/voice/voice-anchor-NN.wav` with only the parameters needed for this workflow:

- `Type: "tts"`;
- `Lang`: `"zh"`, `"en"`, `"ja"`, `"ko"`, `"de"`, `"fr"`, `"ru"`, `"pt"`, `"es"`, or `"it"`, matching the intended spoken language when supported;
- `Text`: for a supported language, a natural neutral passage long enough to extract a complete 3-second timbre sample at the accepted speaking rhythm, unrelated to the product, brand, selling points, campaign, CTA, price, actual script, or any planned video line;
- `VoiceDesc`: the accepted timbre description **including the relevant baseline tone, energy, emotion, and rhythm in which that neutral passage should be spoken**;
- `OutputPath`: the absolute candidate anchor path; objective duration and technical checks below must pass, then the user must hear and accept the presented sample before it becomes an accepted reference.

The current TTS tool has no duration parameter: `DurationSec` applies only to `Type: "music"`, and `Duration` is not a TTS parameter. Do not pass either to force a 3-second sample. A short `Text` or a duration request in `VoiceDesc` does not prove the actual file's duration.

Do not pass unrelated or legacy fields. In particular, do not use this TTS call to synthesize any real line from the video. A suitable neutral passage may describe ordinary light, weather, time, or a daily observation; it must not be reusable as narration, dialogue, monologue, or marketing copy. Its delivery must nevertheless use the confirmed role- and campaign-relevant tone rather than a generic flat reading. After the Three-Second Samples gate below passes, present the actual technically valid audio sample through `present_sandbox_file` with its user-facing role name, explain that its words are only a neutral sample while that role's voice and overall speaking tone are being confirmed, then confirm all affected roles through a closed `dynamic_questionnaire`. If `present_sandbox_file` fails or is unavailable, report that the sample could not be presented, keep the voice gate open, and do not ask whether the unheard sample is suitable. If adjustment is selected after successful presentation, collect the affected role names and concrete changes in a focused follow-up. Regenerate only rejected roles, roles whose samples cannot satisfy the objective duration/technical gate, or roles whose identity or baseline tone changed materially.

If the intended spoken language is outside the supported `Lang` list, use `"en"` as the carrier language and a non-lexical vocal sample such as `Ah, ha, wa, la, oh.` Do not translate a real line into English and do not use target-language words. Include in `VoiceDesc` that the voice is speaking neutral open syllables, while preserving the accepted baseline tone, energy, emotion, and rhythm. This fallback anchors the performer's timbre and baseline delivery only; it does not prove target-language pronunciation or accent, which remain the responsibility of the later video-generation call.

Keep this distinction explicit: **the sample words are unrelated, the sample performance is relevant, and the finished video's shot-by-shot delivery still follows the script**. For this newly synthesized neutral anchor, omit `AudioList` and every other optional field not listed above; define the intended voice and campaign-relevant delivery directly in `VoiceDesc`.

Every written spoken line must identify both its speaker and speech type: `Narration`, `Dialogue`, or `Monologue`. Never leave a multi-speaker line under an unlabeled generic speech field. The video blueprint must include a speaking-role table that maps each role name to its stable semantic voice ID, voice character, and baseline delivery; then each shot assigns each line to one of those exact role names. It must not preassign `Audio[N]`. The executable direct/part mapper assigns final audio positions only after its ordered `AudioList` is known. Do not invent a voice for a role that has not been mapped and confirmed.

For every `sandbox_generate_video` call using concrete voice evidence (including process-required lowered anchors), build `AudioList` from the accepted anchors for **all and only the speaking identities whose lines occur in that call**. Keep a deterministic order and translate the plan's semantic IDs into one-based mappings in the executable prompt, for example `Audio[1] is only the timbre reference for the narrator; Audio[2] is only the timbre reference for the character "Xiaolin"`. Reattach the same exact role anchor in every direct call, lowered part, retry, or later batch video where that role speaks. Each attached sample is timbre-only: never quote, transcribe, paraphrase, continue, or reuse its neutral words, timing, or duration. The actual wording and who says it come only from the current video blueprint or compiled lowered input; the line-specific tone, emotion, emphasis, and pacing remain authoritative. Repeating a path, semantic ID, or role name inside prompt text does not attach audio; only the actual ordered `AudioList` does.

Before execution, include the number, type, measured durations, and total duration of the exact required audio inputs in the target capability check, under the per-call gate below. If the selected target cannot accept audio references, cannot accept enough anchors, or cannot reliably distinguish several referenced voices, disclose that hard limitation before generation and do not promise multi-speaker or cross-call voice consistency. Do not silently merge characters into one voice to fit a limit; obtain user confirmation for any creative change.

### Three-Second Samples

Every timbre reference sample used by this skill must be exactly 3 seconds: before presentation the candidate must decode, contain non-silent audio, avoid objective clipping/truncation defects, and satisfy `duration_seconds == 3.000` (`duration_ms == 3000`); after presentation the user decides whether its voice and delivery are suitable. This applies to new samples, retries, replacements, and previously generated anchors reused after recovery. This is a timbre-reference duration requirement, not a duration rule for authored narration, music, ambience, a character's actual spoken lines, or the finished video.

1. Generate an unrelated neutral passage at the accepted natural rhythm, then inspect the actual output with `ffprobe` (or an equivalent reliable media probe). For example, `ffprobe -v error -show_entries format=duration -of json "$AUDIO"` reads the resolved actual file. Use its measured duration, not text length, requested duration, filename, or an old unbound status value. Unknown, non-finite, shorter-than-3-second, or longer-than-3-second final samples cannot pass. Do not round a short sample up to 3000ms.
2. If longer than 3 seconds, preserve the original and use `sandbox_bash` with `ffmpeg` to extract a coherent, representative excerpt of exactly 3 seconds to a fresh WAV path. For example, `ffmpeg -nostdin -n -i "$SOURCE" -ss "$START_SECONDS" -t 3 -vn -c:a pcm_s16le "$NEW_WAV"` uses actual resolved paths and a reviewed start point. Verify that the output path is absent before invoking it, and verify actual new output rather than trusting exit status alone. Never overwrite the source, speed up speech, or change pitch to fit. If the source is shorter than 3 seconds, do not use it as the final reference or merely pad silence to disguise insufficient source material. Generate a longer neutral passage and take a complete 3-second excerpt; preserve the accepted timbre and natural rhythm.
3. Probe the exact final file again and inspect it for objective defects before presentation: reject silence-only, undecodable, or visibly/audibly truncated candidates. The model may describe concerns and propose a different trim or regeneration, but it must not declare that the voice quality or role suitability has passed. If no technically valid 3-second excerpt exists, resolve any required paid-call authorization and generate a suitable neutral passage; do not loop blindly. Present the duration-checked candidate under the existing role-specific gate and let the user accept or revise its subjective voice and delivery.
4. Report the final absolute path in a paths list through the ready `provide-audio` typed action only after objective probing, successful `present_sandbox_file`, and user confirmation. Preserve source path, selected range and superseded relationship in project artifacts or conversation evidence. A new audio path requires new measurement; acceptance of a long original does not approve the extracted file. Keep the accepted `VoiceDesc` unless the user changes it; a revision automatically makes affected downstream evidence stale.

User-provided timbre references must also meet the 3-second requirement before attachment, but are never silently trimmed, padded or resynthesized. Obtain authorization for a new 3-second excerpt from a longer source, or request a suitable replacement for a shorter source; preserve the original and use the existing review/revision gates. User-provided audio serving other declared purposes follows its own duration needs and the per-call total below.

### Per-Call Reference Audio Budget

Immediately before every video call, including direct execution, each lowered part, later batch videos, and retries, probe or verify measured duration evidence for every file in the exact ordered `AudioList`. Sum the actual durations of all entries, including user-provided audio and repeated entries; never count only newly generated samples, deduplicate the payload for arithmetic, or round durations down. The sum must be strictly `< 15.000` seconds. A disclosed stricter target limit also applies. Check every timbre anchor's exact 3-second duration independently; a valid total does not excuse either a short or an overlong sample.

Four 3-second anchors total 12 seconds and pass this duration budget; five total exactly 15 seconds and fail. This is a per-call budget, not a per-batch role limit, and duration success does not waive count/type/voice-distinguishability constraints. Include the ordered per-file durations, their total, file identities, and pass/block result in the ready `pass-preflight` evidence and the exact payload artifact.

If any duration is unknown/stale, a timbre sample is not exactly 3 seconds, or the sum is at least 15 seconds, stop before calling `sandbox_generate_video`. Repair only the affected samples or evidence, then recompute the actual payload. If all required voices still cannot fit, use the existing hard-constraint/target-lowering workflow after execution selection; for visual packages, every split script still needs its matching recomposed sheet. If lowering cannot preserve the agreed content, ask for a compatible execution choice or explicit creative revision. Never drop a speaking role's anchor, merge distinct voices, alter speech speed, or shorten valid samples arbitrarily just to evade the total-duration gate.
