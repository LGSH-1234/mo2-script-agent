# Battle-mode specificity example

This example demonstrates detail level and shot variety. It is not a fixed story. Replace every style-dependent element with the selected card.

Draw example: `450010 = 60 × 7500 + 10`, so card 11 is Surrealism; `7500 mod 12 = 0`, so mode 1/A is Impact Battle.

## Production-ready 15-second prompt

```text
IDENTITY LOCK
Character A is the exact user-supplied full-body IP character. Preserve facial geometry, eye spacing and color, nose, mouth, hair silhouette and part, body proportions, outfit cut and layers, footwear, accessories, emblem, signature palette and outline treatment in every shot. Do not add armor, weapons, jewelry or exposed anatomy not visible in the reference. Character A remains the same person in every shot even when the selected medium changes the surface rendering.

Opponent B is a non-human, non-graphic Surrealist construct: a tall bone-white chess-tower body with one detached graphite shadow shaped like an angular lance. Preserve the same tower silhouette, scale and shadow-lance direction across all shots. Opponent B has no human face, blood, wounds or realistic body.

STYLE LOCK
Surrealist art space rendered as hyper-real impossible imagery with a pale cerulean sky, endless ochre ground, graphite grey, rust orange and bone white; ultra-smooth matte oil surfaces, photographic sharpness, deep focus, crisp impossible cast shadows, vast negative space, extreme scale shifts and deadpan eye-level logic. Gravity and shadow connection may fail calmly, but the palette, hard light, smooth medium and empty horizon remain identical across all art-space shots.

TIMELINE
0.00–1.20s — Extreme close-up, 100mm macro feel. Character A's eye and one signature hair or face anchor fill the frame. Rack focus moves from the eye to the reflection of Opponent B's graphite shadow-lance sliding toward camera across the iris. Character A makes one sharp inhale; lips stay closed. Crisp hard light, no haze.

1.20–2.90s — Crash pullback to a 24mm extreme wide shot at eye level. Character A stands small on the ochre plain; Opponent B towers thirty meters away, its bone-white body motionless while the detached shadow-lance races forward independently. The pullback stops abruptly, making the geography and scale readable. A low dry impact pulse begins.

2.90–5.05s — Low lateral tracking shot moving left to right. The flat shadow-lance slices across the ground toward Character A. Character A sprints three steps in the same screen direction, plants one foot and slides beneath the passing shadow edge; hair, coat tails or ribbons lag naturally. The lance misses the body and strikes the ground behind Character A with a sharp stone crack. No body contact.

5.05–6.90s — Hard cut to a top-down orthographic shot. The impact folds the ochre ground into three rigid paper-like planes, creating a triangular trap around Character A. Character A rolls once into the open gap and ends facing Opponent B; the same hand remains empty before and after the roll. Crisp graphite shadows preserve the selected style.

6.90–9.45s — Medium arc shot rotating forty-five degrees around Character A at increasing speed. Character A steps on the tip of the detached shadow-lance, pins it to the ground and sweeps one foot in a tight circle. The arc movement and foot sweep redirect the shadow like a solid ribbon around Opponent B's base. Hold Character A's face readable for several frames at the midpoint before acceleration resumes.

9.45–12.20s — Low-angle push and short crane upward. Character A pulls both hands apart without holding an invented weapon; the redirected shadow snaps upward under Opponent B. The giant tower rises weightlessly, rotates once in complete silence, then compresses into a tiny bone-white chess piece that drops safely toward Character A. The camera accelerates upward with the reversal and stops before the landing.

12.20–15.00s — Match cut from the falling miniature to a locked medium-wide shot in the original ordinary room. Character A catches the tiny chess piece in the same hand position, places it on a real table and steps back. The figure remains still, but its graphite shadow taps twice against the tabletop from inside the object. Hold Character A's unchanged face, full silhouette and the moving carried-back shadow for the final 1.8 seconds.

CONTINUITY
Character A travels left to right through the attack and faces right during the counter. The empty-hand state is preserved until the miniature safely falls into the hand. Shot 4 ends with Character A facing Opponent B, matching the opening orientation of Shot 5. The graphite detached shadow is the only motif carried back to reality.

SOUND
One inhale, a fast dry shadow hiss, stone crack, folding-ground creaks, two foot plants, a bass impact pulse, complete silence during the weightless reversal, a light porcelain-like catch and two tiny taps on the real tabletop. Music uses fast low-string ostinato from 1.20s to 9.45s, one rising brass-and-percussion accent for the reversal, then cuts to room tone for the final taps.

NEGATIVE CONSTRAINTS
No identity drift, face replacement, hairstyle change, costume change, new weapon, new armor, extra limb, merged hand, foot sliding, inconsistent screen direction, second art style, changing palette, changing light logic, generic glowing portal, random particle explosion, blood, wound, penetration, dismemberment, realistic suffering, chaotic unreadable melee, camera shake that hides action, or motion blur covering Character A's face.
```
