# 15-second action and camera patterns

Choose the pattern that best fits the asset and selected style. Adapt the transition and environmental reactions to the selected card. Do not reuse the same pattern on consecutive rerolls when a previous result is known.

The numeric order is A=1 through L=12. The default mode number is `(draw quotient mod 12) + 1`.

Asset compatibility:

- full-body or rigged asset: A–L
- half-body asset: B, E, F, I, J, K or L; A only when the battle uses upper-body gestures and style-native forces rather than running or kicking
- portrait asset: E, I, K or L
- flat single image: I, J, K or L with parallax, panel, print, light, texture or camera motion instead of invented limb articulation

When the derived pattern is incompatible, advance cyclically to the next compatible letter and report the change.

## A. Impact battle · 7 shots

- 0.0–1.2s — extreme close-up or macro: Character A notices the style-native threat; rack focus to one identity anchor.
- 1.2–3.0s — rapid pullback to wide geography: establish Character A, Opponent B and the world rule.
- 3.0–5.2s — low lateral tracking: the opponent attacks; Character A evades with one readable path.
- 5.2–7.2s — overhead or high angle: the attack changes the floor, pattern, pigment, shadow or architecture.
- 7.2–9.8s — medium arc shot: Character A counters; hold the face for several frames at the midpoint.
- 9.8–12.5s — low-angle push or fast crane: one decisive non-lethal finishing action redirects the world's own rule.
- 12.5–15.0s — locked wide tableau: opponent safely neutralized; one transformed motif returns to reality.

Use attack → evade → environmental consequence → counter → reversal → finish. Make the clash violent in rhythm and spatial force, not graphic in injury.

## B. Kinetic comedy · 6 shots

- 0.0–1.5s — deadpan medium shot of an ordinary action.
- 1.5–3.2s — macro insert of the prop breaking the world's rule.
- 3.2–5.5s — snap zoom out revealing the absurd scale change.
- 5.5–8.5s — handheld or lateral tracking as Character A tries to recover.
- 8.5–11.8s — overhead reveal of a larger visual consequence.
- 11.8–15.0s — locked reaction close-up followed by a clean loop cue.

The joke must work muted. Use anticipation, delayed reaction, balance, contact and timing instead of captions.

## C. Relentless chase · 7 shots

- 0.0–1.0s — macro clue or shadow enters frame.
- 1.0–2.8s — whip pan to Character A starting the run.
- 2.8–5.0s — side-on tracking with clear screen direction.
- 5.0–6.8s — POV through the style-native threshold.
- 6.8–9.2s — overhead geography reveals shortcut and pursuer.
- 9.2–12.0s — front-facing backward tracking as the environment closes in.
- 12.0–15.0s — abrupt static wide: Character A stops safely while the pursuer overshoots or transforms.

## D. Dance and rhythm transfer · 5 shots

- 0.0–2.0s — full-body static frame establishes costume, feet and beat.
- 2.0–5.0s — 35mm lateral tracking follows the first phrase.
- 5.0–8.0s — top-down or graphic frontal shot lets the selected world react to footwork.
- 8.0–11.5s — medium arc follows the fastest turn while preserving face and hand count.
- 11.5–15.0s — low wide hero landing; secondary motion resolves and creates a loop.

Keep the original rhythm and pose order when a reference video is supplied, but do not copy its performer, clothing or location.

## E. Puzzle and discovery · 6 shots

- 0.0–1.8s — macro insert of an impossible clue.
- 1.8–4.0s — over-shoulder view establishes the puzzle space.
- 4.0–6.5s — top-down composition reveals pattern relationships.
- 6.5–9.0s — close profile with rack focus as Character A acts on one insight.
- 9.0–12.0s — slow crane or pullback reveals the world reconfiguring.
- 12.0–15.0s — symmetrical wide result with one unresolved but enticing detail.

## F. Stealth and near miss · 7 shots

- 0.0–1.3s — detail of moving light, shadow or pattern.
- 1.3–3.0s — compressed long-lens close-up of Character A listening.
- 3.0–5.2s — low tracking behind foreground cover.
- 5.2–7.0s — Opponent B POV scans past Character A.
- 7.0–9.3s — overhead shot clarifies the safe path.
- 9.3–12.0s — slow dolly as Character A crosses the last exposed gap.
- 12.0–15.0s — close-up release, followed by the carried-back motif betraying the escape.

## G. Sport or impossible trick · 6 shots

- 0.0–1.5s — macro preparation: shoe, hand, wheel, ball or style-native object.
- 1.5–3.5s — low wide takeoff with full body visible.
- 3.5–6.0s — side tracking follows acceleration.
- 6.0–8.0s — overhead shows trajectory and world-rule obstacle.
- 8.0–11.5s — controlled slow-motion arc at the single peak moment.
- 11.5–15.0s — normal-speed landing, medium reaction and wide final hold.

## H. Rescue under pressure · 6 shots

- 0.0–1.5s — extreme close-up of the endangered object or creature.
- 1.5–3.8s — wide shot establishes distance and hazard.
- 3.8–6.2s — forward tracking follows Character A entering the hazard.
- 6.2–8.8s — side medium shot shows the one precise rescue action.
- 8.8–11.5s — crane up as the world rule reverses or collapses safely.
- 11.5–15.0s — intimate close-up, then pull out to reveal the rescued motif following home.

## I. Transformation performance · 5 shots

- 0.0–2.2s — neutral face-readable medium shot in reality.
- 2.2–5.0s — orbit or vertical tilt as the environment transforms first.
- 5.0–8.0s — macro-to-medium match cuts apply the selected medium to clothing surfaces without changing design.
- 8.0–11.5s — full-body reveal uses the style card's native camera behavior.
- 11.5–15.0s — iconic held portrait; one visual layer peels away to show identity remains unchanged.

## J. Ritual or performance space · 5 shots

- 0.0–2.0s — symmetrical or frontal establishing tableau.
- 2.0–4.8s — macro hand gesture or prop contact starts the world rule.
- 4.8–8.0s — circular or lateral camera movement reveals repeating environmental response.
- 8.0–11.5s — overhead pattern locks the character to the composition.
- 11.5–15.0s — slow push into a final face-readable icon.

## K. Living-object encounter · 6 shots

- 0.0–1.5s — macro object detail moves on its own.
- 1.5–3.5s — reaction close-up preserves Character A's face.
- 3.5–6.0s — low two-shot establishes scale and distance.
- 6.0–8.5s — POV or object-mounted camera during the interaction.
- 8.5–12.0s — wide reveal shows the object's effect on the art space.
- 12.0–15.0s — quiet medium final beat where Character A brings one harmless trait home.

## L. Reality-glitch escalation · 6 shots

- 0.0–1.5s — perfectly level ordinary wide shot.
- 1.5–3.2s — insert of one impossible mismatch.
- 3.2–5.8s — repeat the first composition with a larger violation.
- 5.8–8.5s — rotating, sliding or folding camera move native to the selected style.
- 8.5–11.8s — extreme wide reveals the whole world obeying the anomaly.
- 11.8–15.0s — return to the opening frame except for one carried-back error.

## Camera selection rules

- Use at least four distinct shot sizes or viewpoints in 15 seconds.
- Use at most two aggressive moves such as whip pan, fast orbit, crash zoom or strong handheld shake.
- Preserve the 180-degree axis unless an overhead, frontal reset or visible axis crossing establishes the reversal.
- Do not hide every cut behind blur, flare, smoke, particles or a foreground wipe.
- Reserve at least 1.5 seconds for the final readable consequence.
- Style-native flat movements may replace 3D camera travel with panel changes, layered planes, print registration, page turns or graphic scale changes.

## Prompt assembly contract

Return the final 15-second prompt in this order:

1. `IDENTITY LOCK` — Character A's immutable anchors, outfit and any opponent lock.
2. `STYLE LOCK` — the selected card's style, light, palette, composition, medium, mood, camera behavior and world rule in one reusable paragraph.
3. `TIMELINE` — every shot with exact start and end time, shot size, angle, lens behavior, camera movement, subject action, environmental reaction, transition and sound cue.
4. `CONTINUITY` — screen direction, held objects, final-to-next pose matching and carried-back motif.
5. `NEGATIVE CONSTRAINTS` — identity drift, style drift, anatomy errors, unsupported props, generic portals and action-specific failures.

The prompt must describe observable motion rather than adjectives alone. Replace “intense fight” with a readable attack path, evasive path, impact point, environmental deformation, counter, reversal and finish. Replace “cinematic camera” with the exact shot size and camera move.
