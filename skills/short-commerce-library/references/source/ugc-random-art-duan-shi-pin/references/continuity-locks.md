# Character and style continuity locks

## Character identity bible

Extract only traits visible in the supplied asset. Divide them into two groups.

### Immutable anchors

- facial geometry: face width, jaw, cheek shape, eye spacing, eye shape, iris color, nose and mouth
- hair structure: silhouette, length, part, fringe, tied sections and signature highlight placement
- body design: apparent proportions, head-to-body ratio and limb scale visible in the source
- outfit design: garment type, silhouette, layers, closures, trim and footwear visible in the source
- signature marks: accessories, emblem, scars, horns, ears, tail or other IP-defining features

Repeat at least five high-value anchors in the global lock and every high-risk shot. Never invent unseen anatomy, hidden costume details, jewelry or weapons.

### Style-adaptable surface

The selected movement may change line quality, pigment, brush texture, print texture, shadow shape, color treatment and environmental light spill. It may not change the immutable anchors. At least three anchors must remain plainly readable in every art-space shot and five in the final held frame.

## Three-phase transition lock

1. **Reality:** preserve the source rendering and ordinary physical light.
2. **Threshold:** transform environment first, light second, medium third; keep the face and silhouette unchanged.
3. **Art space:** render the character through the selected medium while retaining exact identity geometry and costume design.

Do not morph the face as a transition effect. Do not use smoke, motion blur, flare or particles to hide an identity failure.

## Selected-style bible

Turn the chosen card into one sentence containing its style, light, palette, composition, medium, mood, camera behavior, native threshold and world rule. Reuse that sentence as the global style lock.

Across all art-space shots:

- use one palette hierarchy and one light logic
- preserve the same medium and edge treatment
- preserve the same spatial rule and transition material
- keep camera behavior compatible with the card
- do not mix in a second named movement
- do not revert to generic photorealism, generic anime or generic fantasy between cuts

Reality may remain neutral, but every carried-back motif must visibly retain the selected movement's material, color and physical rule.

## Multi-shot continuity

- Label the user character `Character A` in every shot.
- If an opponent appears, label it `Opponent B` and lock its silhouette, scale, material and screen direction separately.
- Record wardrobe, held objects and left/right hand occupancy.
- Record entry and exit screen direction for every shot; reverse it only after a visible turn or axis-crossing shot.
- Match the final pose of one shot to the starting pose of the next when using motion cuts.
- Use one neutral face-readable shot before and after the fastest movement.
- Keep the same carried-back motif until the final reveal.

## Battle continuity

Intensity must not destroy legibility. Establish Character A and Opponent B in a wide or medium-wide shot before their first clash. Use no more than one main attack, one counter, one reversal and one finishing action in 15 seconds.

Keep attacks non-graphic and stylized. Show force through environmental deformation, light, paper, pigment, geometry, shadow, wind or debris native to the selected movement. Do not show blood, wounds, bone impact, prolonged suffering or realistic weapon penetration.
