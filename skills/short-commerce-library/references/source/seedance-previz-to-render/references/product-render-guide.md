
# Product Render Guide

Use this guide when the uploaded source is a product white-model, rough-model presentation video, product reveal, product turntable, product hero shot sequence, or commercial render-upgrade request.

The key challenge in product tasks is precision. Product rendering fails when the result is visually noisy, materially inconsistent, or commercially unreadable.

## Main objective

Turn a structurally useful but visually incomplete product source into a premium Seedance 2.5 render proposal that preserves the reveal logic while upgrading:
- material credibility,
- surface precision,
- reflection control,
- lighting discipline,
- background support,
- commercial polish.

## Product-first principles

### 1. Preserve reveal logic
If the white-model already encodes:
- rotation,
- reveal order,
- opening mechanism,
- hero angle,
- detail close-ups,
- assembly sequence,
keep that structure whenever possible.

Do not replace a clear product demo structure with random cinematic flourishes.

### 2. Surface credibility matters most
For products, weak material definition is often more damaging than weak atmosphere.

Always clarify:
- what each surface is made of,
- whether it is matte, satin, glossy, brushed, polished, anodized, coated, rubberized, translucent, or fabric-based,
- how clean or used it should feel,
- how edge transitions behave.

### 3. Control reflections deliberately
Product renders often break when reflections are generic or chaotic.

Define:
- reflection sharpness,
- reflection softness,
- reflection scale,
- highlight placement,
- whether reflections should emphasize curvature, premium finish, or technical precision.

### 4. Commercial readability beats unnecessary drama
If the user wants premium advertising quality, keep the product easy to read.
Avoid excessive darkness, excessive atmospheric haze, or camera language that hides the key form.

## Product render checklist

Always reason through:
- silhouette clarity,
- surface separation,
- material contrast,
- branding zone clarity,
- detail legibility,
- reflection discipline,
- support environment cleanliness,
- premium finish level.

## Material guidance by common product category

### Consumer electronics
Focus on:
- brushed or polished metal,
- glass finish,
- coated plastic,
- speaker mesh / fabric detail,
- precision seams,
- soft specular gradients,
- clean background separation.

### Wearables
Focus on:
- skin-adjacent material realism,
- strap texture,
- metal casing finish,
- display glass clarity,
- edge highlight control,
- subtle luxury cues.

### Beauty / packaging
Focus on:
- lacquered surfaces,
- translucency,
- label/readability zone preservation,
- controlled studio reflections,
- premium smoothness,
- clean hero staging.

### Industrial / hardware products
Focus on:
- machining quality,
- industrial material honesty,
- durable surface treatment,
- weight and solidity,
- functional detailing,
- less decorative, more engineered lighting.

## Lighting strategy for products

Choose one lighting logic and keep it coherent.

### Premium commercial studio
Use for:
- electronics,
- wearables,
- luxury goods,
- cosmetics,
- hero product reveal.

Typical traits:
- soft controlled key,
- clean specular lines,
- deliberate rim shaping,
- pure or minimal background,
- premium high-finish polish.

### Dramatic premium
Use when the user wants:
- stronger impact,
- more luxury mood,
- darker high-contrast feel.

Typical traits:
- deeper shadow structure,
- tighter controlled highlights,
- stronger silhouette contrast,
- selective visibility.

### Tech-futurist
Use for:
- devices,
- AI hardware,
- gaming gear,
- future-facing products.

Typical traits:
- cool-toned highlights,
- reflective geometry emphasis,
- sharper light accents,
- more designed environment support.

## Background and support logic

The background should support the product, not compete with it.

Decide whether the right answer is:
- clean gradient sweep,
- premium abstract environment,
- branded graphic minimal environment,
- soft atmospheric studio,
- high-tech dark reflective stage.

Do not add clutter unless it supports the product story.

## Prompt emphasis for products

When writing the final prompt, make sure it includes:
- exact product material behavior,
- edge highlight behavior,
- reflection style,
- reveal pacing,
- background discipline,
- finish quality,
- commercial clarity.

## Common mistakes

Avoid:
- overusing cinematic grit on premium products,
- mixing too many materials without logic,
- vague wording like “高端科技感” with no surface detail,
- hiding the product shape behind atmosphere,
- excessive wide-angle distortion,
- inconsistent reflection direction,
- overly busy environment design.

## Output hint

In product cases, the strongest render proposal usually makes it obvious:
- why the material choice fits the product,
- how the light reveals form,
- how the background supports readability,
- why the final direction is commercially stronger.
