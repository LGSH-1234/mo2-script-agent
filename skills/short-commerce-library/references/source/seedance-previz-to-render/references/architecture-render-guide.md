
# Architecture Render Guide

Use this guide when the source is an architecture white-model walkthrough, interior white-model, exhibition route previs, spatial demo video, or any rough source centered on built environment presentation.

The core challenge in architecture and spatial tasks is credibility. A beautiful result that breaks spatial logic, lighting logic, or material plausibility is weaker than a quieter but believable render.

## Main objective

Transform rough spatial walkthrough material into a strong Seedance 2.5 render proposal that preserves:
- spatial layout,
- route logic,
- circulation clarity,
- scale readability,
- camera movement intent,
while upgrading:
- material credibility,
- light logic,
- atmosphere,
- finish quality,
- spatial layering.

## Architecture-first principles

### 1. Preserve circulation logic
If the source already defines a route through the space, preserve that route.
Do not introduce camera behavior that breaks orientation or makes the space feel smaller, larger, or more confusing without reason.

### 2. Credible materials matter more than decorative style
In architecture tasks, weak material plausibility quickly damages the result.
Always clarify:
- wall finish,
- floor finish,
- ceiling logic,
- glass type,
- metal type,
- stone / wood / concrete behavior,
- edge detail,
- junction realism,
- scale-appropriate texture density.

### 3. Lighting must feel motivated
Architecture renders fail when light feels arbitrary.
Identify whether the space is primarily shaped by:
- daylight,
- skylight,
- practical interior lighting,
- accent lighting,
- exhibition lighting,
- mixed conditions.

The light should reveal the space, not merely decorate it.

### 4. Atmosphere should reinforce spatial depth
Use haze, bloom, glow, reflections, and ambient softness only when they enhance:
- depth,
- material reading,
- mood,
- circulation clarity.

Do not add cinematic fog just because it looks dramatic.

## Architecture render checklist

Always reason through:
- spatial hierarchy,
- circulation clarity,
- scale readability,
- material realism,
- daylight or practical-light logic,
- floor/wall/ceiling differentiation,
- reflection logic,
- architectural atmosphere,
- camera smoothness,
- finish credibility.

## Material guidance for spaces

### Wall systems
Consider:
- painted wall,
- plaster,
- concrete,
- stone cladding,
- wood paneling,
- acoustic treatment,
- exhibition wall finish.

### Floor systems
Consider:
- polished stone,
- matte stone,
- wood flooring,
- carpet,
- resin floor,
- industrial concrete,
- tile,
- gallery floor finish.

### Glass and metal
Clarify:
- transparency level,
- reflection control,
- frame material,
- brushed vs polished metal,
- display hardware precision.

### Furniture / set elements
If present, specify whether they are:
- minimal support elements,
- hero design objects,
- circulation cues,
- exhibition supports.

## Lighting strategies for spaces

### Natural daylight realism
Use for:
- residential,
- hospitality,
- office,
- public space,
- calm premium architectural presentation.

Typical traits:
- believable sun or skylight direction,
- soft interior fill,
- gentle contrast,
- natural material reading.

### Practical-light enriched interior
Use when practical fixtures matter.
Typical traits:
- fixture motivation,
- warm/cool balance,
- layered pools of light,
- stronger atmosphere in evening or controlled interior scenes.

### Exhibition / gallery enhancement
Use for:
- exhibit routes,
- branded spaces,
- installations,
- future-facing public environments.

Typical traits:
- controlled focal accents,
- route guidance,
- clean material emphasis,
- presentation-led atmosphere.

## Camera guidance for walkthroughs

Preserve:
- path logic,
- scale comprehension,
- orientation,
- movement smoothness.

Enhance carefully:
- lens discipline,
- stabilization feel,
- reveal pacing,
- spatial compression or expansion only if useful,
- focal emphasis at key moments.

Do not turn architecture walkthroughs into chaotic cinematic chase shots.

## Prompt emphasis for spatial tasks

When writing the final prompt, make sure it clearly includes:
- material identity by zone,
- lighting motivation,
- spatial layering,
- route preservation,
- camera smoothness,
- atmospheric subtlety,
- finish realism.

## Common mistakes

Avoid:
- making materials too glossy everywhere,
- using unmotivated dramatic contrast,
- adding haze that reduces space readability,
- ignoring architectural scale,
- over-stylizing a space that should feel credible,
- cluttering the space with unnecessary objects,
- using character-action language for environment tasks.

## Output hint

In strong architecture proposals, the user should clearly understand:
- how the material system was completed,
- why the light feels believable,
- how the route and camera are preserved,
- why the chosen direction improves the space without breaking it.
