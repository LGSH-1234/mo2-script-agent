
# Character Previs Guide

Use this guide when the source is a character motion-blocking clip, action previs, fight white-model, chase previs, narrative blocking sequence, or any rough source centered on character movement and shot rhythm.

The main challenge in character previs tasks is balance: preserve action readability and shot structure while upgrading atmosphere, realism or stylization, costume/material logic, and cinematic force.

## Main objective

Transform rough character previs into a stronger Seedance 2.5 render proposal without losing:
- blocking clarity,
- movement rhythm,
- camera intent,
- spatial geography,
- dramatic cause-and-effect.

## Character-first principles

### 1. Respect movement readability
If the action becomes hard to read, the render upgrade has failed.

Always preserve or improve:
- silhouette clarity,
- body direction,
- impact beats,
- line of action,
- spacing between characters and environment,
- spatial comprehension.

### 2. Preserve shot rhythm
If the white-model already contains a strong sequence of:
- wide setup,
- pursuit movement,
- impact close-up,
- reaction beat,
- directional cut,
respect that sequence.

Do not replace functional action grammar with decorative rendering language.

### 3. Materials must serve motion
Character rendering is not only about face or costume beauty.
Ask:
- does the costume material support movement readability?
- do props and surfaces help separate the figure from the background?
- does texture add useful realism without visual clutter?

### 4. Atmosphere should support clarity, not bury it
Fog, rain, particles, smoke, and dramatic lighting are useful only if they strengthen the action and mood without obscuring the blocking.

## Character render checklist

Always reason through:
- main subject readability,
- body silhouette,
- costume material logic,
- skin/hair realism or stylization,
- prop material logic,
- environment support,
- contrast structure,
- action emphasis,
- emotional tone,
- depth separation.

## Material guidance for character previs

### Costume
Specify:
- fabric type,
- stiffness vs softness,
- reflectivity,
- weathering or cleanliness,
- layering,
- premium realism vs stylized simplification.

### Skin / face / hair
Only specify what matters for the intended finish.
Examples:
- realistic skin texture,
- stylized smooth finish,
- controlled sweat level,
- hair edge detail,
- beard/stubble or clean finish,
- subsurface softness when appropriate.

### Props and weapons
Clarify:
- material class,
- wear level,
- edge quality,
- highlight behavior,
- whether props are hero objects or support elements.

## Lighting strategy for action scenes

### Dramatic directional lighting
Use when the user wants high contrast, tension, stronger emotional mood.
Best for:
- fight scenes,
- chase scenes,
- night action,
- dramatic confrontation.

### Readable cinematic realism
Use when clarity is more important than spectacle.
Best for:
- tactical movement,
- choreography-heavy scenes,
- scenes where staging and geography matter.

### Stylized impact lighting
Use when the user wants stronger visual punch, game-trailer energy, or heightened CG style.
Be careful not to lose motion readability.

## Environment support

The environment in character previs should help the character read clearly.
Add:
- depth layers,
- practical light cues,
- debris or particles if they strengthen action,
- atmospheric separation,
- impact marks or surface response when appropriate.

Do not overload the background so much that the character disappears into it.

## Camera and shot enhancement

In character previs, the camera is part of the storytelling.
Preserve:
- directional momentum,
- pursuit trajectory,
- impact emphasis,
- scene geography,
- escalation rhythm.

Enhance carefully:
- lens feel,
- focus transitions,
- depth separation,
- camera intensity,
- motion smoothness or intentional aggression.

## Prompt emphasis for character previs

When writing the final prompt, emphasize:
- action clarity,
- silhouette readability,
- costume and prop material logic,
- atmosphere as support,
- dramatic or readable light strategy,
- camera rhythm,
- emotional energy.

## Common mistakes

Avoid:
- adding so much atmosphere that the action becomes muddy,
- making every shot over-stylized,
- ignoring the original shot grammar,
- replacing movement logic with random visual flair,
- generic “电影感” wording with no action-specific detail,
- over-detailing minor costume elements while neglecting silhouette,
- using product-style polish on scenes that need grit and energy.

## Output hint

In strong character previs proposals, the user should be able to tell:
- which action beats are preserved,
- how the render makes the action easier to feel,
- how materials and light support performance,
- why the recommended direction is best for both clarity and impact.
